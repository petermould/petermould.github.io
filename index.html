Computer vision · in progress

Cricket Bowling Analysis

A pose-estimation pipeline for cricket bowling biomechanics. I hand-annotated 96 frames in CVAT to fine-tune a YOLO11-pose model on a custom shoulder-elbow-wrist skeleton, then built the analysis layer from scratch — elbow angle from joint-vector geometry, wrist speed normalised against the delivery's own peak (the source footage's true frame rate is unknown, so speed is reported relative rather than in m/s). Renders as an annotated video with live-updating angle and speed graphs.

About

Most have never run the system on a matchday.

I work freelance as a systems operator at Hawkeye Innovations. On a matchday that means the setting up and calibration of a multi-camera tracking system responsible for the Goal-line technology across UEFA and UK proffesional football.

That job taught me the part of this field you can't pick up from a dataset: camera geometry, high pressure environments, calibration and teamwork — and what actually happens when a tracking system meets rain, floodlights and a full stadium. It's the same problem space as the computer vision work above, just approached from the operations side first.

Since then I've been building the analytical half: YOLO detection and tracking pipelines, a five-league scouting database rated with K-means clustering and TOPSIS, and a Monte Carlo forecasting model I published before a tournament rather than after it. I'm looking for a full-time role in sports analytics or sports technology.
